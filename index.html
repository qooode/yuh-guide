Match Frame Rate

Where
  1. Settings
  2. Video and Audio
  3. Match Content
  4. Match Frame Rate
Set to
On
Why

Films are made at 24 pictures per second. If the Apple TV always sends 60 Hz, those pictures can't be spread evenly, so camera pans look jerky. That's called judder. With this on, the Apple TV switches to the film's own rate.

24pictures per second in a film
60refreshes per second on a 60 Hz TV
2.5refreshes per picture. Half a refresh isn't possible.
Match Frame Rate offTV runs at 60 Hz
Judder

Each picture held for 3, 2, 3, 2… refreshes. Uneven.

Match Frame Rate onTV runs at 24 Hz
Smooth

Each picture held for exactly 1 refresh. Even.

Picture on screen now One TV refresh Numbers = film pictures · slowed down 6×

Watch the two squares. Top: the TV has to show some pictures longer than others, so the square moves in a long-short-long-short rhythm. On screen, that's the stutter you see in slow pans. Bottom: every picture gets the same time, so it moves evenly.

Not working well for you?

No single setting works for everyone, since TVs and apps vary. Native frame rate output is generally recommended to avoid A/V issues, and it's one of the Apple TV's strengths.

If your TV doesn't handle the signal correctly, or your streaming app doesn't support frame matching, turn this off and let your TV handle the pulldown instead.

Continuous Audio Connection

Where
  1. Settings
  2. Video and Audio
  3. Audio Format
  4. HDMI Output
Set to
Off
Why

New in tvOS 26.4. When on, the Apple TV holds a constant Dolby MAT connection to your receiver or soundbar. Non-Atmos audio is packaged as 7.1.4 LPCM at 16-bit / 48 kHz with silent objects, so your system always sees what looks like an Atmos stream. It fixes some problems and causes others.

What it fixes
  • No dropouts or delays when switching between stereo, 5.1 and Atmos.
  • No volume jumps between stereo, Dolby Digital Plus and Atmos, a common LPCM issue on some systems.
  • No interruptions in Apple Music, or when one track is Atmos and the next isn't.
  • Can fix lip-sync issues over eARC.
  • Can fix audio pops or crackling on some systems.
What it breaks
  • Most receivers and soundbars treat the output as true Atmos at all times, which can cause incorrect channel behavior or altered surround processing.
  • Upmixing (Dolby Surround, DTS Neural:X and others) may stop working. There's no upmixing from an Atmos source, and upmixing from 7.1.4 may be faulty.
  • Atmos is always sent compressed at 16-bit instead of 24-bit. That's an Apple TV limit either way, but with this on, lossless audio (from Infuse, for example) is affected too, which can reduce dynamic range.

Tested with a 7.2.4 AVR setup, a 3.0 AVR setup and a 3.1.2 soundbar.

Bottom lineIt's not set-and-forget, since the result depends on your system. Unless you need one of the fixes above, leave it off.

Why

A matter of taste, but off keeps the original dynamic range of films and shows. Quiet parts stay quiet, loud parts hit properly.

Troubleshooting

Volume feels too low?

A common complaint. The Apple TV converts all audio to LPCM, and some receivers and soundbars handle the volume level (dialnorm) differently. In your receiver or soundbar settings, raise the volume for the HDMI input your Apple TV is connected to.

Last resort

Older system without LPCM?

Go to Settings › Video and Audio › Audio Format › Change Format and choose Dolby Digital. Older systems can then play the audio, but quality drops. Only do this if nothing else works.

04Dolby Vision

Nothing to change here, just helpful background. Dolby Vision comes in several "profiles" built from different layers, and that's what decides how each one plays on the Apple TV.

P5

Streaming

Dolby Vision metadataDolby Vision base layer

Used by every major service. No HDR10 fallback, so unsupported devices show green or pink. The Apple TV plays it with excellent color accuracy.

Best on Apple TV
P8

Hybrid

Dolby Vision metadataHDR10 base layer

Supports both HDR10 and Dolby Vision. Converted from P7 or P5 for compatibility, and most third-party players handle it. Slightly less accurate color than P5, though few will notice.

Most compatible
P7

UHD Blu-ray

Dolby Vision metadataEnhancement layerHDR10 base layer

Dual-layer format from discs, like remux files. The Apple TV can't use the enhancement layer, so Odin plays the HDR10 base layer instead.

Falls back to HDR10
